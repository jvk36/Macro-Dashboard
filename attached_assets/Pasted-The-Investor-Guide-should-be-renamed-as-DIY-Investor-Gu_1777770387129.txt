The “Investor Guide” should be renamed as “DIY Investor Guide” and should have the following Sections with the given info: a) How To Read Each Indicator – 1) PMI (Purchasing Managers’ Index) - A survey of corporate purchasing managers. Above 50 = expansion, below 50 = contraction. One of the fastest leading indicators — released on the first business day of the month. Compare manufacturing vs. services to see where the economy is bifurcating. Source: ISM (US) · S&P Global (global) · Released: Monthly, 2) Yield Curve (2s10s spread) - the difference between the 10-year and 2-year Treasury yields. When it's negative (inverted), it has historically preceded every US recession by 6–18 months. Not a timing tool — inversion can persist for 12–24 months before a downturn hits. Source: Federal Reserve / Treasury · Watch: Daily, 3) Nonfarm Payrolls (NFP) - Released the first Friday of every month. The most market-moving data release. Above ~150K = healthy labor market. More important than the headline: watch average hourly earnings (wage inflation) and the U-6 underemployment rate. Source: BLS · Released: First Friday of month, 4) PCE vs. CPI — what's the difference? - The Fed targets PCE, not CPI. PCE adjusts for consumer substitution (if beef gets expensive, people buy chicken), so it tends to run 0.3–0.5% lower than CPI. Core PCE (ex-food & energy) is the single most important inflation number for Fed policy decisions. Source: BEA · Released: Monthly, ~4 weeks after month-end, 5) Credit spreads - The extra yield investors demand to hold corporate bonds over Treasuries. High-yield (junk) spreads above 500–600bps have historically signaled recession stress. Tightening spreads = risk-on; widening spreads = risk-off. Watch HY spreads as an early warning system for equities. Source: ICE BofA index · Bloomberg/FRED · Watch: Weekly, 6) Leading vs. lagging indicators - Leading: PMIs, LEI, yield curve, building permits, jobless claims — signal what's coming. Coincident: payrolls, industrial production, retail sales — confirm current state. Lagging: GDP, unemployment rate, CPI — confirm what already happened. Build your view using leading indicators; validate with coincident data. Classification: Conference Board framework, b) Data Release Calendar – a table with the following rows and columns (Frequency, Key Releases) – 1) Weekly - Initial jobless claims (Thu), Baker Hughes rig count (Fri), etc., 2) Monthly - NFP (1st Fri), CPI (mid-month), Retail sales, JOLTS, PCE, PMIs, Housing starts, 3) Quarterly - GDP (advance → revised → final), FOMC dot plot, Corporate earnings, etc., 4) As Needed - FOMC decisions (8×/yr), IMF WEO (Apr/Oct), Fed Beige Book (8×/yr), etc.
